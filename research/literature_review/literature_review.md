# Literature review and novelty comparison matrix

Status: Stage 2. Date: 2026-11-06.

Method and limits. Entries were looked up with web search this session; bibliographic details marked "verified" matched at least one publisher, proceedings, arXiv or DBLP page. Mechanism descriptions come from the abstracts and summaries returned by search, not from reading every full paper, and that is stated where it matters. "Cited from memory" means I know the work but did not re-check it this session. This is a targeted prior-art search, not a systematic review. It cannot establish that nothing similar exists. ## 1. Families of prior work and what each actually computes

**A. Pairwise graph learners.** GCN (Kipf and Welling, ICLR 2017) propagates features over normalised adjacency, a first-order spectral approximation. GAT (Veličković et al., ICLR 2018) replaces fixed weights with learned attention over neighbours. HGT (Hu, Dong, Wang, Sun, WWW 2020) makes attention parameters depend on node and edge type and adds relative temporal encoding. All three aggregate over *pairs* (edges). A set of three skills supports a competency only through repeated two-body messages. Whether the true effect is irreducibly three-way is not modelled or testable inside these architectures.

**B. Hypergraph neural networks.** HGNN (Feng et al., AAAI 2019) convolves over a hypergraph Laplacian built from hyperedge incidence. HyperGCN (Yadati et al., NeurIPS 2019) approximates hyperedges by weighted pairwise edges (spectral hypergraph theory). AllSet (Chien et al., ICLR 2022) writes a layer as two multiset functions, node-to-hyperedge and hyperedge-to-node, and shows many hypergraph propagation rules fit this form. Hyper-SAGNN (Zhang, Zou, Ma, ICLR 2020) scores *tuples* of nodes by self-attention and handles variable hyperedge size. NHP (Yadati et al., CIKM 2020) predicts unseen hyperlinks inductively. These models take hyperedges as *given structure* or score a candidate tuple as a black box. None decomposes the score into main, pairwise and higher-order parts, and none is built for partially labelled, multi-label "does this set support competency c" with expert-reviewed negatives.

**C. Knowledge tracing and cognitive diagnosis.** DKT (Piech et al., NeurIPS 2015) uses an RNN over interaction sequences. GKT (Nakagawa et al., WI 2019; journal version Web Intelligence 19, 2021) casts knowledge components as graph nodes. NeuralCD (Wang et al., AAAI 2020) replaces hand-designed interaction functions by a neural network with a monotonicity assumption. **G-DINA** (de la Torre, Psychometrika 76(2), 2011) is the most relevant psychometric precedent: an item's success probability is a saturated function of the required attributes, with parameters for main effects and for each attribute interaction, and DINA, DINO, R-RUM and additive CDM as constrained special cases. So *"success needs a combination of skills, with interactions"* is established in psychometrics. What G-DINA lacks for PLN: per-item free parameters (no generalisation to unseen skills or items), a Q-matrix given a priori, and no cross-domain or unknown-label setting.

**D. Learning-path recommendation.** KnowLP (Cheng et al., AAAI 2026, vol. 40(17), 14610-14620) builds prerequisite and similarity graphs with a GraphRAG module and a reinforcement-learning module that switches to similar concepts when prerequisite progress stalls. FKGRec (Fan, Tong, Li, Information Processing and Management 63(2), 104393, 2026, per DBLP) adds a forgetting-aware graph network (MemGNN) and an RL reward balancing new and previously learned concepts. Both recommend *existing* concepts in a *given* graph. Neither discovers new higher-order competencies from skill combinations. (The reported results of both are authors' claims; I have not reproduced them.)

**E. Set-function and interaction mathematics.** A set function on skills can be written uniquely in the Möbius basis: f(S) = sum over T within S of m(T). A **k-additive** capacity restricts to |T| <= k (Grabisch, Fuzzy Sets and Systems 92(2), 1997, DOI 10.1016/S0165-0114(97)00168-1). The Shapley and pairwise interaction indices follow from m (Grabisch and Roubens, Int. J. Game Theory 28(4), 1999, DOI 10.1007/s001820050125). Choquet-integral models for multi-criteria decision aid use this machinery (Grabisch and Labreuche, Ann. Oper. Res. 175, 2010); NEUR-HCI (Bresson et al., IJCAI 2020) is a neural architecture for hierarchical 2-additive Choquet integrals. Faith-Shap (Tsai, Yeh, Ravikumar, JMLR 24(94), 2023) defines a faithful higher-order Shapley interaction index through polynomial approximation, as a *post-hoc explanation* of a fixed model. Wendler et al. (AAAI 2021) learn set functions sparse in non-orthogonal Fourier bases, which include Möbius-type bases, from queries. Factorization machines and **higher-order FMs** (Rendle, ICDM 2010; Blondel et al., NeurIPS 2016) model order-m feature interactions with low-rank parameters and the ANOVA kernel evaluated by dynamic programming in O(d m) time.

**F. Interaction identifiability and hierarchy.** Purifying Interaction Effects with the Functional ANOVA (Lengerich et al., AISTATS 2020) shows that main and interaction effects can trade contribution without changing predictions, and defines *pure* interactions as variance no subset of features explains. Hierarchical lasso (Bien, Taylor, Tibshirani, Ann. Stat. 41(3), 2013, DOI 10.1214/13-AOS1096) encodes strong and weak heredity: an interaction is allowed only if its main effects are present.

**G. Positive-unlabeled learning.** Elkan and Noto (KDD 2008, pp. 213-220): under random selection of labelled positives, a classifier trained on positive vs unlabeled predicts probabilities that differ from the true posterior by a constant factor. nnPU (Kiryo et al., NeurIPS 2017) adds a non-negative risk estimator that stops flexible models overfitting the unbiased estimator. Bekker and Davis (Machine Learning 109, 2020, DOI 10.1007/s10994-020-05877-5) survey PU learning and define the selected-at-random (SAR) setting where labelling propensity depends on attributes.

**H. Set-input neural networks.** Deep Sets (Zaheer et al., NeurIPS 2017) characterise permutation-invariant functions as rho(sum phi). Set Transformer (Lee et al., ICML 2019) uses attention blocks with pooling by multihead attention over seed vectors.

**I. Data sources.** ESCO (EU) has 13,485 skill concepts in its skills pillar and credits O\*NET content under CC BY 4.0 on its site (verify each licence directly before ingest). Both describe *occupational* associations, not demonstrated learner competence.

## 3. The most important unresolved computational problem

The literature gives (i) hypergraph and set networks that *score* sets as black boxes, (ii) psychometric and decision-theory models that decompose set effects exactly but fit small, fixed, expert-specified problems, and (iii) education models that work over pairwise graphs. What I could not find in this search is a model that jointly does all of the following for PLN:

1. Scores *arbitrary variable-size skill sets* across domains, including sets and skills **unseen in training**, through embedding-generated coefficients.
2. Splits the score **exactly** into individual, pairwise and higher-order parts so that "irreducibly higher-order" is a falsifiable statement (refit at k=2 vs k=3), not an interpretation.
3. Treats **unknown labels as unknown**, with reviewed negatives kept distinct, per competency.
4. Reuses the **same function** for discovery (binary skill sets) and learner readiness (mastery probabilities), with exact propagation of mastery uncertainty.

"Could not find in this search" is not "does not exist". Each of the four ingredients is individually established (items 1, 2 and 4 largely by G-DINA, k-additive capacities, HOFM, PU learning). Their combination into one trained, evaluated system for cross-domain competency discovery is the only candidate contribution, and it is an **unverified research hypothesis**.

## 4. What the PLN proposal's own cited work does not settle

The proposal asks whether a model can learn that a *combination* of competencies creates the foundation for another. KnowLP and FKGRec recommend along or around given prerequisite and similarity structure; neither claims to find unrepresented competencies. That gap is real in what I read, but I read abstracts and summaries, not the full papers.
